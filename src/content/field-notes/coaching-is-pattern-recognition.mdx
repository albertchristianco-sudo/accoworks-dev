---
title: "Coaching Is Pattern Recognition"
date: "2026-05-30"
label: "Training"
summary: "A note on why good coaching is less about collecting cues and more about seeing the pattern in front of you."
---

Good coaching starts before the cue.

Most people think coaching is about knowing the right thing to say. Knees out. Brace. Finish tall. Push the floor away. Those can help, but the cue is only the visible part of the work.

The real skill is pattern recognition.

You watch how someone moves when they are fresh, then when they are tired, then when they are frustrated. You see what changes under load. You notice whether the issue is strength, position, fear, attention, or just a person trying too hard to perform instead of learning.

That is why the same cue can fix one athlete and confuse another.

A beginner may need fewer words. A competitor may need sharper language. Someone rebuilding confidence may need the next rep to feel safe before it feels strong.

The job is not to sound smart. The job is to make the next rep better.

I think this is also why coaching has shaped how I operate outside the gym. Business has patterns. Teams have patterns. Systems break in patterns. People rarely fail randomly. Something in the environment, the incentive, the instruction, or the expectation is usually pulling them there.

The better you see, the less you need to shout.
