title: "The IT Guy Was There First"
date: "2026-09-05"
label: "Building"
summary: "Before the gyms, before CrossFit, I was the IT guy. The AI side is that old chapter coming back."
---

Most people who know me now only know one version of me.

Coach. Gym owner. The guy yelling numbers at you during a WOD. Meathead, basically, and I'll say that one myself. I spent years building that reputation on purpose.

Then they find out I build AI agents. Actual software: Python, APIs, systems running in the background of my house and my businesses.

From the outside, that doesn't add up. A coach who codes sounds like a plot twist, like I picked up a strange hobby somewhere between snatch cycles.

What they're missing is that before the fitness, before the gyms, before CrossFit entered my life, I was the IT guy. I studied Information Technology at the University of San Carlos. I fixed computers, set up networks, and did tech support for myself and everyone in my friend group. That came first.

Fitness came later and took over the stage. The tech never left. People just stopped seeing it.

I've never paid an IT expense in any business I've run, Subtero and CrossFit included. Not for a website, not for graphic design. I did it all myself. People assume gym owners hire that stuff out. I never did, because the IT guy was in the room the whole time.

For a while, AI didn't change any of that. Two years ago it was a chatbot, and I played with it like a toy. I ran Dungeons and Dragons campaigns through it, built characters, and let it improvise a dungeon master voice. It was fun. Then I parked it, the way you park any toy.

What pulled me back was the shift to agents. A chatbot answers you. An agent gets a goal and a set of tools, and it carries out the task on its own. Once I saw that, the old knowledge came back. I went back to Python and HTML, stuff I'd assumed was a closed chapter. Turns out I was just waiting for a reason that mattered.

So when people are surprised that a gym coach builds agents, I get it. From where they stand, it looks like two unrelated things stapled together. To me it's the IT guy coming back. He was there first.
