Why now

The pain is operational before it is technological.

The Philippines already knows the justice system needs faster, more accessible, and more technology-adaptive operations. The private legal market has the same problem, only closer to the client.

Court and legal work move through documents.

Pleadings, affidavits, demand letters, contracts, board documents, permits, employment files, compliance notices, and due diligence packs create the daily burden. AI is strongest where work is text-heavy, repetitive, and reviewed by a professional before release.

80% Target share of first-draft and first-review labor that AI can compress before attorney review. The target is leverage, not replacement.

SMEs avoid preventive legal work.

Many small businesses only call a lawyer after a dispute, resignation, unpaid invoice, lease issue, or government notice. A lower-cost legal subscription can move them earlier in the risk curve.

Cebu has local-context needs.

Real estate, construction, BPO subcontracting, hospitality, trading, gyms, clinics, schools, and family businesses need practical contracts and compliance, not abstract legal research tools.

Trust is the buying factor.

The product should not pretend to give independent legal advice. It should present itself as attorney-supervised legal operations, with AI used for speed, document control, and consistency.

Niche options

Three niches are worth testing first.

Do not start with a general AI lawyer app. Start with a narrow, frequent, painful business problem where an attorney can review output and charge immediately.

Selection criteria

A strong wedge should be recurring, document-heavy, low drama, high trust, and easy to explain to Cebu business owners.

SME Legal Subscription

Document intake, title checklisting, contract risk flags, seller or developer document review, and clean due diligence reports for buyers, brokers, and SMEs acquiring property.

Guardrails

The ethics posture must be conservative.

The venture should be built as professional legal service delivery enhanced by AI, not as unsupervised legal advice.

Human lawyer remains accountable.

The attorney verifies citations, facts, legal conclusions, and final documents. AI output is treated as junior work product, never as authority.

Confidentiality and privacy first.

Client documents should be handled under a clear data processing policy, with restricted tools, no public model training on client files, and Data Privacy Act controls from day one.

No unauthorized practice line-crossing.

The site should state that services are attorney-led. AI does not create an attorney-client relationship by itself, and no automated answer should be represented as final legal advice.

Audit trail matters.

Keep source documents, prompts, generated drafts, attorney edits, and final versions. This protects quality, improves templates, and gives the lawyer professional control.

Package the service around practical business risk: contracts, demand letters, employment documents, basic corporate housekeeping, Data Privacy Act readiness, and compliance reminders. AI handles intake, first drafts, comparison, summaries, and checklists. The attorney reviews and signs off.

Execution path

Build the service before building the software.

The first version should be a premium, manually operated service with AI inside the workflow. If clients pay repeatedly, then productize.

Weeks 1-2

Offer design

Name the packages, define covered documents, approval rules, turnaround times, and excluded legal matters.

Weeks 3-4

Workflow build

Create intake forms, document templates, review checklists, source verification steps, and client folders.

Weeks 5-8

Pilot accounts

Recruit 10 trusted Cebu businesses. Measure document volume, attorney review time, common risks, and willingness to pay.

Weeks 9-12

Productize

Turn repeated work into a portal, client dashboard, template library, and compliance calendar only after usage is proven.
