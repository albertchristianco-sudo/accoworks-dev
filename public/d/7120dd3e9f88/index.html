“An active operations assistant that only asks management to intervene when something needs attention.”

Your framing of the goal — it anchors this whole plan.
What the system is

One living record per shipment

Every shipment gets a single record that lives from the moment you expect it to the moment the file is closed — billing done, documentation done, final pickup done. Before an entry number exists, the record keys off the BL number, container number, client reference and vessel/voyage; once the VASP issues the BOC entry number, that becomes the principal customs reference — exactly as you work today.

Expected→ Arriving→ Arrived→ For Lodgement→ Lodged→ In customs→ Released→ Delivered→ Billed / Closed

Everything in one place

Client, cargo, mode (sea or air), origin, vessel and voyage, containers, port, remote handler, lane, exceptions, and the full status picture — no more cross-referencing sheets.

A complete history

Every status change, lane assignment, handoff, note and correction is recorded with who made it and when. The record can always answer “what happened, and when?”

Board and detail views

The whole operation on one board — filter by department, client, port, lane, mode or target status; search by reference, BL, entry number, container or vessel. Click any shipment for the full picture.

How status works

Three status views, side by side

You told us shipment status and customs status are separate, connected fields — not one pipeline, and not a forced sequence. So every shipment carries three independent status views that move at their own pace. A vessel can be delayed while the paperwork is ready; an entry can be with the examiner while delivery is already being coordinated.

How status works

The customs lane, front and center

You asked for the lane to be a first-class, highly visible field — not a note in a remarks column. Every shipment shows its lane on the board, in the detail view, and in reports.

Lane reporting, as you specified

  • How many shipments are in each lane right now
  • Each lane’s share of your entries over any date range
  • Average time from lodgement to release, by lane

Lane changes are tracked, too

When a lane is assigned or changed, the system records when — so lane timing reports reflect what actually happened, not a guess after the fact.

Target tracking

The five-day clock

From the moment an entry is lodged, the clock runs. Every shipment shows its lodgement date and time, target release date, days elapsed, days remaining — and once released, the actual release date next to the target.

Every miss gets a reason — and an owner

A shipment beyond target must carry a reason for delay, and the delay is attributed: DCA-controlled, or Customs, SPS, shipping line, client, or documentation. The reports then split your performance into what was yours to fix and what was not — the distinction you asked for.

The calendar knows the Philippines

Philippine national holidays are built in, and the counting rules are settings, not hard-wired: whether the five days count working days or calendar days, and how early “approaching” should warn. Two of those choices are still open with you — they’re on the final page.

Exception management

Problems get owned, dated, and never lost

Your exception taxonomy, exactly as you gave it — each one a proper category, not a remarks note:

SPS Issue Customs Examination Missing Documents Valuation Issue Permit Issue Shipping Line Issue Client Issue Payment / Debit Issue Telex Release Delay Other Customs Hold

Every exception carries

  • A category and a severity
  • A responsible department — and optionally a named person
  • A due date
  • A resolution note when it closes

Nothing quietly disappears

An open exception stays visible on the shipment’s card, in the detail view, and on the dashboard until someone resolves it. The full open-and-resolve history stays on the record.

SPS gets special weight

Because frozen and agricultural cargo is a real part of your volume, SPS issues always surface on the management dashboard the moment they are opened.

The management dashboard

Continuity

Your words, and your history, come with you

The system speaks DCA

Statuses, lanes and exception categories use your exact terms — Lodged, Selected, Final, Debited, Released, and the rest. They are settings, not programming: rename one, add one, or retire one whenever your workflow changes, and every past record keeps its history intact.

Day one is not a blank screen

Your existing tracker is imported before go-live, with a preview step so you can see exactly what will come in. The board starts populated with your live and historical shipments, and every imported record is marked as imported — so you always know where a record came from.

Corrections are safe. Key dates — arrival, lodgement, release, delivery — are stamped automatically as statuses change, and never overwritten by accident. If a date needs correcting, management can correct it, and the correction itself is recorded with the reason. The record stays honest.

Trust

Your data is yours alone

01

Fenced, completely

DCA’s shipments, clients, and figures live in their own fenced space. The fence is enforced at the deepest level of the system — not by a setting that could be switched off.

02

Nothing vanishes

Shipments, their histories and their exceptions are never deleted — records are retired, not erased. The history of every shipment is permanent and cannot be edited after the fact, by anyone.

03

Management holds the keys

Settings — statuses, lanes, targets, rules, members — can only be changed by owner and admin accounts. Staff accounts work the shipments; they don’t reshape the system.

When the automatic customs connections arrive in a later phase, the same principle extends to your VASP credentials: they are never stored in the system’s database, and no automated access runs without your written go-ahead. More on that on the next page.

Sequence

The build, phase by phase

Sequence first, dates second — each phase stands on the one before it, and the tracker is the foundation everything later plugs into.

PHASE 1

The tracker

The working system: every shipment, three status views, lanes, the five-day clock, exceptions, the management dashboard, reports, settings — with your existing tracker imported. Stands alone; no outside connections yet.

PHASE 2

Viber assistant

Ask for a shipment’s status and record updates from Viber, and management receives exception alerts there — on top of the same records.

PHASE 3

Customs connections

InterCommerce, CDEC and eKonek feed customs status automatically, in your stated order of preference: official API, authorized feed, automated portal access, manual fallback.

PHASE 4

Shipping-line tracking

Vessel and container status arrives on its own. The right data source is one of the open items on the next page.

PHASE 5

A window for your clients

A clean tracking view your customers can check themselves — built on the same records, when you decide you want it.

On the customs connections, your hard constraint is ours too: nothing may put your credentials or BOC accreditation at risk. So — credentials are never stored in the system; automated access stops itself rather than ever risk locking a real account; every automated access is logged; and no automated login runs at all without your written authorization.

Open items

Five things we’d like from you

None of these hold up the start of the build — the tracker is designed so each answer can be set when it arrives. They shape the settings and the later phases.

1

VASP advices today

Do InterCommerce, CDEC or eKonek already send your office email or SMS advices when an entry moves — Selected, Final, Debited, Released? If they do, those advices could become an early data source.

2

Shipping-line information

Where should vessel and container status come from? For example: do the shipping lines send arrival notices to your office today, or should we look at the lines’ own tracking channels?

3

The five-day count

Should the five-day target count working days or calendar days? The system supports both; we’ve set working days as the starting point until you confirm.

4

“Approaching” the target

How many days before the target date should a shipment start showing as Approaching Target? It’s a setting — we’ve started it at two days out until you tell us otherwise.

5

Your highest-volume VASP

Of InterCommerce, CDEC and eKonek — which carries most of your entries? Its connection gets built first when the customs-connection phase begins.

Answer these any time — in a call, by message, or one at a time. Each one slots straight into a setting or a phase decision. Nothing waits.
